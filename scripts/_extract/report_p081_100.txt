5.4 Preprocessing Module          
The main challenge of all-weather gate operation is tackled in the preprocessing module. License 
plate detection and vehicle make –model recognition are affected by the images captured under 
fog, rain, low light conditions, having reduced contrast, blurred plates and lower vehicle texture. 
To counteract this, three specialised classical-vision pipelines are used: fog removal, rain removal 
and night/low-light enhancement. Each branch is used prior to the AI models, and the downstream 
weights of YOLO and EfficientNet are the same.     
5.4.1 Fog Removal Pipeline          
The fog removal pipeline uses the Dark Channel Prior (DCP) filter, which is based on the 
minimum intensity value across the RGB (R, G, B) channels. In the RGB, brightness and 
color information are combined together.    
• Dark channel estimation with a morphological kernel of size 13. Dark channel 
estimation with 13×13 morphological kernel.    
• The darkest 0.1% of the image's pixels are used to estimate the atmospheric light. The 
method assumes that the darkest 0.1% of the image's pixels represent the atmospheric 
light.

• The transmission map computation and refinement is done with a guided filter (radius =  
50).    
• Scene radiance recovery: Reconstruction of dehazed color.    
• Removal of residual haze over bright areas with low variance.    
• Gray world white balance to correct color cast    
• Adaptive gamma correction (γ  [0.6, 1.8])    
• CLAHE applied to the luminance channel of CLA VE (2.5, 8×8 tiles)    
• This is a mild HSV saturation boost, ×1.2.    
• Edge restoration with bilateral denoising and unsharp masking.

5.4.2 Rain Removal Pipeline          
The rain removal pipeline is designed to remove rain from vertical rain streaks without 
destroying plate and text regions. It consists of:    
• Two smoothing filters (bilateral smoothing) and Canny edge detection are used to create 
a plate/text protection mask.    
• Multiscale vertical median filtering (kernel sizes 15, 11 and 7) + brightness-rejection 
blending (to prevent halos of characters)    
• Unwanted detection of rain spots in locally bright areas.    
• Continue to use Telea inpainting (radius = 3) for the remaining streak artifacts outside 
protected areas. It is used to fill damaged pixels and missing pixels in an image

5.4.3 Night / Low-Light Enhancement Pipeline         
The night and low-light enhancement pipeline is designed to restore detail to plates and vehicles despite 
the lighting conditions including IR-style night lighting, deep shadow, head light glare and street lighting. 
A pipeline has the following components:    
\n===== PAGE 93 =====\n

• Adaptive black-level lift for very dark frames, in LAB luminance channel. The Lab color 
space consists of three components: L*, a*, and b*. L* represents lightness, while a* 
represents the green–red color range and b* represents the blue–yellow color range. This 
separation of brightness and color makes Lab useful in image processing tasks like 
enhancement and segmentation.    
• CLAHE in LAB space, adaptive clip limit (4.5–8.5) and 6×6 tiles.    
• Excellent adaptive gamma correction (γ  [0.28, 0.62]) to boost shadows.    
• The brightness gain, when intensity is below 100, is achieved globally.    
• Additional boost of HSV saturation (×1.55) to enhance faded colors.    
• Glare and blur recovery by means of unsharp masking: to recover edges of plates and 
vehicles that have been lost due to glare or blur.

6.1 Summary          
This thesis introduced an All-Weather Smart Vehicle Gate Access System, which is a multistage artificial 
intelligence pipeline that automates vehicle access control by combining license plate detection, 
character recognition, vehicle make and model classif ication, weather adaptive image pre -processing, 
edge deployment on Orange Pi 5 Pro and a cloud-based administrative web dashboard.    
The system was designed as a modular system, with each module trained, validated and tested 
separately before being integrated into the end -to-end system. The complete pipeline involves the 
preprocessing of the image for weather conditions (fog, rain or night/low-light), recognition of make 
and model of the vehicle using EfficientNet -B4, detection and cropping of the license plate using 
YOLOv8n, character recognition for each character using another YOLOv8n model, reconstruction 
of the plate text to the Pak istani ABC-1234 format and finally, an allow/deny decision based on 
crossreferencing the plate and vehicle identity with the registered vehicle database. Results will be 
synced to the Vercel -hosted dashboard, where you can monitor them in real -time, log th em, and 
manage them.         
6.2 Key Achievements          
1. License Plate Recognition: A fine-tuned YOLOv8n model on the Roboflow License Plate Project 
v1 dataset (20,580 images for training) validated 98.07% mAP at 0.5 with 98.50% precision and   
95.53% recall on held out data.    
2. Character-Digit Recognition: A YOLOv8n model for character recognition was trained on 1235 
annotated images across 38 classes with 97.7% mAP@0.5, 99.0% precision, and 94.2% recall 
(validation was done on the training split and no separate validation folder was exported).    
3. Vehicle Make and Model Recognition: 3.9% Top-1 error and 0.6% Top-5 error on a held -out test 
set of 17,254 images with an efficientnet-b4 trained on 57,135 images across 245 vehicle classes, was 
achieved with 96.18% macro F1-score and 0.02% macro FPR.    
4. Under adverse weather conditions: In fog, rain, and night/low-light scenarios, three classicalvision 
pipelines (Dark Channel Prior –based Fog Removal, multiscale vertical median filtering with Telea 
inpainting for rain, and LAB-CLAHE night/low-light enhancement) preserve the recognition quality 
of images.    
5. To ensure within project's ≤2 s latency requirement, all 3 AI models are deployed locally on Orange  
Pi 5 Pro (16 GB) without cloud inference, with the latency of about 700-1000 ms per frame on CPU.    
6. Web Dashboard: Node.js/Express API deployed on Vercel with a responsive React 18 /   
TypeScript / Vite / Tailwind CSS dashboard, real -time monitoring (5 -second auto-refresh), vehicle event logging, 
manual override, temporary visitor passes, weather display and rolebased access control.    \n===== PAGE 100 =====\n 
86    
    
7. Integrated Pipeline: All modules were integrated into a single and end -to-end pipeline, which was 
dual modality authorized (with plate and vehicle model), synchronized with cloud events through 
gate_sync.py and qualitatively demonstrated in fog, rain and low-light scenarios.

6.4 Limitations          
• Generalization of region: Models were trained on the license plate style of the region mainly in the 
case of Pakistan and the common vehicle make/models in the region. Results are not validated for 
other countries, plate formats, or for uncommon cars.    
• Heavy occlusion: Severe motion blur, poor camera focus, or very degraded imagery may also 
impact the confidence of plate detection and vehicle classification, even when processing under 
challenging capture conditions, including fog, rain, and night preprocessing.    
• Physical gate actuation: Integrated with barrier-motor, allow and deny decisions can be automated, 
logging to the cloud, and optionally indicating on GPIO, but further integration with industrial 
safety certification is not part of this FYP cycle and would  require site specific hardware 
installation.
