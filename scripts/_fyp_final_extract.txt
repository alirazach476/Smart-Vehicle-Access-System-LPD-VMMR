Abstract    
This project presents a Smart Vehicle Gate Recognition System designed to automate 
vehicle access decisions in gated communities and parking facilities while reducing reliance 
on manual checks. The system integrates YOLO-based license plate detection, optical 
character recognition (OCR), and convolutional neural networks for vehicle make and model 
classification. The pipeline of image preprocessing is used to improve the lowlight images, 
decrease the noise, diminishes the fog and glare and refines the edges of characters to 
make YOLO V8 more strong in the real scenarios. The classification of vehicles is achieved 
on the basis of a fine-tuned EfficientNet-B0 model, and the ResNet18 is applied to 
benchmarking based on transfer learning. The decision to grant an authorization is made by 
comparing known license plates and vehicle models projected in an authorized database 
and the visualization of results is implemented on the basis of a web-based dashboard of 
dynamic control and administration. In the work of the future, physical gate control is also to 
be provided.    
CHAPTER 01 — Introduction  
1. Introduction  
Vehicle identification is a basic necessity of a controlled access environment like a parking 
place, residential complex, and safe institutional premise. The traditional vehicle gate systems 
are usually based on manual checks or physical credentials that a re not very efficient, have 
high chances of abuse, and cannot be easily operated on a large scale. The latest advances 
in computer vision and deep learning have opened the possibility of automated visual 
classification of vehicles through simple camera dev ices, which represents a beneficial 
alternative to access control based on credentials.  
This Final Year Project (FYP -1) is designed to test a hybrid recognition pipeline that uses 
license plate detection, optical character recognition, and vehicle make and model recognition 
to enhance access control. The system is intended to perform efficien tly under real -life 
conditions such as low light, glare, and poor weather conditions while making correct and 
consistent decisions. The design is scalable and modifiable to fit various facilities; the 
software-based design will enable support for hardware gates and centralized monitoring 
systems in the future.  
1.1 Historical Background  
The creation of intelligent vehicle gate systems has received growing attention in recent years 
due to rises in artificial intelligence and computer vision. Conventional gate entry systems — 
such as manual checking, RFID tags, or barcodes — are usually slow, subject to errors, and 
insecure in the event of losing or damaging credentials. They also struggle to handle crowded 
areas such as parking lots, corporate campuses, toll plazas, and other secure facilities.  
Modern smart gate systems address these shortcomings by integrating automated licence 
plate recognition with deep learning, allowing contactless identification and authorisation in 
real time. Object detection networks like YOLO and yolov8n tools like EasyOCR  can 
accurately identify license plates and extract plate text. Convolutional neural networks for \n===== PAGE 15 =====\n  
Page 15    
vehicle make and model recognition add an additional security layer, ensuring the recognized 
plate matches the actual vehicle.  
1.2 Objectives and Limitations  
The central purpose of this project is to explore automated vehicle access control based on 
computer vision and deep learning. The essential purposes are:  
• Create a real -time YOLO -based license plate detection unit to identify and locate 
license plates in vehicle images.  
• Write an intelligent YOLOv8 pipeline with EasyOCR and pre -processing options 
including denoising, histogram equalization, CLAHE, detail enhancement, and 
sharpening.  
• Detect vehicle types using EfficientNet-B0 for make and model classification.  
• Install authorization logic that cross -checks identified license plate text and vehicle 
model against an authorized vehicles database.  
• Test performance in various conditions (day/night, glare, fog, rain, motion blur) 
targeting above 90% accuracy.  
Limitations:  
• This phase involves software implementation only; no real -time video streaming 
hardware or physical gates are offered.  
• System performance relies on the quality of camera images and can degrade under 
extreme weather or very low light.  
• Hardware integration including IR cameras and gate actuation is planned for future 
project stages.  
1.3 Project Scope  
This FYP-1 is dedicated to the software portion of the smart vehicle gate recognition system. 
It includes the YOLO-based license plate detection module, an EasyOCR-based recognition 
system with adaptive preprocessing, and a convolutional neural network to classify vehicle 
make and model.  
An authorization logic module linked to a database of registered cars is included and tested 
on camera-sourced images. Performance is evaluated using accuracy, precision, recall, and 
F1 score. Hardware-related work — connecting IR cameras and integrating Raspberry Pi — 
is left to be developed later.

CHAPTER 02 — Literature Review  
2. Literature Review  
This chapter covers the most important technologies and research pertinent to automated 
license plate recognition (ALPR), vehicle make and model recognition (VMMR), and intelligent 
gate systems. It discusses object detection networks, YOLOv8, image preproc essing, and 
vehicle classification. An overview of existing commercial and academic ALPR systems is 
provided, and limitations are assessed. This chapter forms the basis of the design decisions 
taken in this project.  
2.1 Related Technologies  
The project leverages up -to-date computer vision and deep learning methods to permit 
automated vehicle access. YOLO networks are applied to detect license plates at high speed 
and accuracy, while EasyOCR is capable of detecting text under different lighting and weather 
conditions. Preprocessing methods including denoising, contrast enhancement, and 
sharpening improve plate readability. EfficientNet-B0 recognizes the make and model of the 
vehicle, providing an added verification layer.  \n===== PAGE 18 =====\n  
Page 18    
2.1.1 Object Detection Networks  
YOLO (You Only Look Once) formulates object detection as a one-stage regression problem 
rather than a two-stage region proposal process. The YOLO family has evolved from YOLOv3 
to the current YOLOv8, with each generation improving speed and detection accur acy. 
YOLOv8 proposes superior backbone architecture, feature network design, and decoding 
improvements [12][13].  
YOLO is especially appropriate for ALPR since license plates are small objects occupying 10– 
30% of the image. Multi-scale feature extraction enables effective detection in real conditions 
at 30–100 FPS on modern hardware. YOLO consistently outperforms old er methods (Haar 
Cascades, selective search) when trained on license plate datasets [14].  
2.1.2 Optical Character Recognition (OCR)  
Two popular approaches exist for ALPR character recognition:  
Tesseract OCR: An open-source engine that integrates pixel and feature-based recognition. 
Tesseract is tunable using Page Segmentation Modes (PSM); PSM 7 (treat image as a 
single text line) is common for license plate recognition, and custom character whitelisting 
enhances reliability [15].  
Deep Learning-Based OCR: Applications like EasyOCR use CRNN (Convolutional  
Recurrent Neural Network) or Transformer-based models. These methods outperform 
Tesseract under difficult conditions (low resolution, blur, occlusion) though at higher 
computational cost [16][17].  
2.1.3 Image Enhancement and Preprocessing  
License plate images in the field frequently suffer from low contrast, motion blur, noise, glare, 
or weather obstruction. Advanced preprocessing algorithms are used to counter these 
challenges:  
• Denoising: Denoising: Bilateral filtering and Non -Local Means (NLM) remove noise 
while preserving edges [19].  
• Contrast Enhancement: Contrast Enhancement: CLAHE enhances visibility in 
lowlight or high-glare situations without halo effects [20][21].  
• Detail Enhancement: Detail Enhancement: Morphological operations and unsharp 
masking clarify characters against the background [22].  
• Sharpening: Sharpening: Kernel -based sharpening (e.g., Laplacian) sharpens 
characters while minimizing noise amplification [23].  
2.1.4 Vehicle Make and Model Recognition (VMMR)  
VMMR is a fine-grained classification problem requiring distinction among hundreds of vehicle 
classes. Deep CNNs such as ResNet and EfficientNet are commonly used [25][26]:  
• ResNet18: ResNet18: A residual network with 18 learnable layers and skip 
connections to overcome the vanishing gradient problem. Used in this project to 
identify common Pakistani vehicles.  
• EfficientNet-B0: EfficientNet-B0: Best suited for edge and real-time use due to high 
accuracy with minimal computation. Transfer learning with ImageNet weights, 
augmented with data augmentation, makes it robust to pose, lighting, and occlusion 
variations [27][28].  \n===== PAGE 19 =====\n  
Page 19    
2.2 Overview of Existing Systems  
2.2.1 Commercial ALPR Systems  
Commercial ALPR systems such as Avigilon, ANPR UK, and Parkeon are common in parking 
lots, toll plazas, and law enforcement. These systems achieve 90–98% accuracy in controlled 
daytime conditions. However, their efficiency reduces significantly in fog, rai n, or glare, and 
dependency on cloud services or dedicated equipment limits flexibility [29][30][31].  
2.2.2 Academic ALPR Research (2023–2025)  
Recent works focus on strengthening real-world performance:  
• Thapliyal et al. (2023): YOLOv5 with Tesseract achieved good speed but poor 
performance in low lighting [32].  
• Li et al. (2023): Segmentation and CNN -based recognition for Chinese plates 
improved performance in difficult scenes but struggled with rain and fog [33].  
• Agarwal et al. (2024): YOLO combined with Transformer -based yolov8n achieved 
layout-independent recognition at higher computational cost [34].  
• Chen et al. (2024): Fusion method combining image enhancement for motion blur and 
rain [35].  
• Semiromizadeh et al. (2025): Added vehicle recognition with 3D attention modules 
enabling two-step checking (plate + model) to enhance security [39].  
2.3 Limitations of Existing Work  
Despite great advances, current ALPR and VMMR systems have several persistent problems:  
1. Weather Degradation: Accuracy may reduce by 20 –40% during fog, precipitation, or 
glare despite preprocessing [36][40].  
2. Partial Vehicle Checking: Most systems only check plates, failing to offer security 
against false or swapped plates [39].  
3. Real-Time Constraints: Advanced YOLOv8 and video tracking algorithms are 
computationally costly and difficult to run on standard hardware [37][38].  
4. Reduced Integration: Studies tend to focus on standalone individual components 
rather than end-to-end gate automation systems [9].  
5. Dataset Bias: Many datasets are biased toward European or Chinese plates, limiting 
generalization to Pakistani or South Asian contexts [41].  \n===== PAGE 20 =====\n

3.4 Low Level Design and Component Selection  
This section outlines the low-level design of the system, detailing the software and hardware

3.4.1 Image Acquisition — IR Camera with IR Illuminator  
The IR camera captures vehicle and license plate images under changing lighting and weather 
conditions. The built-in IR illuminator maintains consistent lighting in dark or dim areas without 
requiring additional lamps. This component was chosen for its ava ilability, affordability, and 
proven effectiveness in ALPR.  
3.4.2 License Plate Detection — YOLOv8 Detection Model  
YOLOv8 is employed for license plate detection based on its real -time inference capability 
and great detection accuracy. It predicts bounding boxes and class probabilities in a single 
forward pass, making it relevant to time-sensitive gate automation tasks.  
3.4.3 Image Preprocessing — OpenCV Image Processing Library  
OpenCV implements preprocessing operations including denoising, contrast enhancement, 
morphological filtering, and sharpening. These functions enhance visibility of license plates 
under extreme environments. OpenCV was chosen for its open -source nature, hi gh 
optimization, and broad community support.  
3.4.4 OCR — EasyOCR Engine  
EasyOCR extracts license plate text from preprocessed images using deep learning -based 
text recognition, making it resistant to font variations, skew, and partial occlusion. It was 
chosen for its high recognition rate in real images and ease of integration with other software 
components.  
3.4.5 Vehicle Classification — EfficientNet-B0  
EfficientNet-B0 classifies vehicle make and model with high accuracy and low computation 
complexity. The compound scaling approach maximizes depth, width, and resolution, 
permitting efficient inference on standard hardware. Testing confirmed it outperforms ResNet 
and MobileNet alternatives with fewer resources.  
3.4.6 Authorization — Local CSV / SQLite Database  
Authorized pairs of license plates and vehicle models are stored in a lightweight local 
database. SQLite or CSV-based storage was chosen for simplicity and ease of deployment in 
FYP-1. More sophisticated database systems may be implemented in later phases.  
3.4.7 Embedded Processing — Raspberry Pi 5  
The Raspberry Pi 5 will run the entire recognition pipeline at the gate, chosen for its 
affordability and ease of installation. Software validation is currently performed through

CHAPTER 04 — Implementation and Testing  
4. Implementation and Testing  
This chapter explains how the smart vehicle gate recognition system is applied in practice. It 
describes the production, testing, and assembly of every software module into the functioning 
pipeline. Experimental results and performance analysis have also b een introduced to 
evaluate effectiveness and reliability under varying circumstances.  
4.1 Software Implementation  
4.1.1 License Plate Detection Using YOLOv8  
License plate detection is conducted using Ultralytics YOLOv8. YOLOv8-Nano (YOLOv8n) is 
fine-tuned on a dataset of vehicle -based license plates. The model consumes input images 
scaled to 640×640 and gives bounding box locations and confidence scores. Built -in 
\n===== PAGE 29 =====\n  
Page 29    
NonMaximum Suppression (NMS) eliminates redundant detections. Cropped plate regions 
are then passed to the preprocessing and yolov8n stages.  
4.1.2 Adaptive Image Preprocessing  
An adaptive preprocessing pipeline is applied to each detected plate region to enhance 
yolov8n accuracy. This pipeline includes denoising, CLAHE contrast enhancement, histogram 
equalization, and sharpening operations. These measures handle low light, glare, rain 
artifacts, and blur while maintaining character edges. The preprocessing stage runs in 
approximately 100–200 ms on standard CPU hardware.  
4.1.3 License Plate Text Recognition  
EasyOCR extracts text from preprocessed license plate images, returning identified 
characters and confidence scores. The extracted text is converted to uppercase, with 
nonalphanumeric characters removed and the result formatted in standard plate format. 
EasyOCR provides reliable recognition across varying plate conditions at low computational 
cost.  
4.1.4 Vehicle Make and Model Classification  
The CNN -based EfficientNet -B0 model classifies vehicles using a custom labeled car 
makeand-model dataset. EfficientNet-B0 demonstrates high accuracy and robustness across 
common vehicle models. A ResNet18 model was also trained for benchmarking comparison. 
Based on evaluation, EfficientNet -B0 is selected as the primary classifier for its superior 
accuracy and stability.  
4.1.5 Authorization Logic  
The authorization component checks vehicle access against a database of registered plate 
and vehicle model pairs. Access is authorized only when both the detected plate number and 
the predicted vehicle model match an existing database entry. If the plate m atches but the 
vehicle model differs, access is denied and flagged as suspicious.  
4.1.6 Visualization and Logging  
The system generates visual overlays on processed images including plate bounding boxes, 
recognized text, and authorization status. Every processed vehicle generates a log record 
with timestamp, plate number, predicted model, and access decision. These log s support 
tracking and subsequent auditing.
