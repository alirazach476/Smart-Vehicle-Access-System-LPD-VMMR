Abstract    
      
The aim of this project is to design a Smart Vehicle Gate Access System that can minimize manual 
checking for gate access in gated communities, institutional campuses and parking areas. The 
system integrates a weather-aware pre-processing front end, a dual-stage YOLOv8n license plate 
recognition (LPR) pipeline and a vehicle make, and model recognizer (VMMR) based on a 
finetuned EfficientNet-B4 convolutional neural network with 245 vehicle models. Three separate 
branches are used for the restoration: a Dark Channel Prior–based dehazing pipeline with guided 
filtering for fog and haze; a multi -scale vertically oriented median filter with edge -aware 
protection and Telea inpainting for rain streak suppression; and a LAB -space Contrast Limited 
Adaptive Histogram E qualization (CLAHE) pipeline for night and low -light enhancement. 
Lightweight, Pakistani-alphanumeric character trained YOLOv8n detectors are utilized for license 
plate localization and 38 -class alphanumeric character detection, and deterministic post 
processing is used to construct region -specific plate tokens. Access is granted via a dual -modal 
policy that cross-references the reconstructed license plate text, and the predicted vehicle make 
and model against a registered vehicle database, adding another l ayer of verification that is not 
susceptible to plate spoofing and plate -vehicle swapping attacks. All three models are deployed 
as edge AI on an Orange Pi 5 Pro (16 GB), and all process the data from a single, onboard USB 
camera, at the gate lane, with a consistent Python inference pipeline, with the recognized text of 
the plate, vehicle ID, weather context, and annotated snapshots sent over WiFi to a production 
React dashboard running on Vercel via a secure gateingest API. The web platform provides live 
feed monitoring, event logging, vehicle registration, administrative override and automated 
allow/deny gate actions based on the registry rules and confidence thresholds. On held -out test 
data, the on-device inference time is around 700–1000 ms per frame on the Orange Pi CPU, and 
achieved a quantitative evaluation result of 98.07% mAP@50 for license plate detection, 98.07% 
mAP@50 for character recognition, and 96.24% Top-1 for vehicle classification, covering a full 
hardware–software pipeline of image captur e in adverse weather conditions, followed by 
character recognition and, finally, access control via the cloud.

             1. Introduction          
In secure areas like car parks, gated communities, business or institutional campuses, an integral 
part of the challenge is vehicle identification. The traditional methods of credential verification 
(manual, RFID authentication, barcode passes) are labour intensive, prone to human error, easily 
misused and hard to scale to high traffic entry points. New technology in computer vision and 
deep learning has enabled the ability to recognize vehicles contactlessly via camera imagery, 
which presents a scalable, auditable solution to physical credential-based access control.    
The Automatic Vehicle Gate Access System proposed in our Final Year Project combines the 
capabilities of detecting license plates, fine -grained vehicle make and model recognition, and 
character recognition from the license plate using the YOLOv3 series. It is designed to be reliable 
in the real world, supporting fog, rain, and low light conditions, with a pre -processing dedicated 
to weather-awareness before being applied to the inference. It enhances security over plate -only 
systems with a dual-modality authorization policy, which cross-referenced discovered text on the 
plate and predicted vehicle identity from a registered database. All three AI models run locally on 
an Orange Pi 5 Pro edge node, data is synced in real time to a production React dashboard hosted 
on Vercel for live monitoring, event logging and administrative control, and the complete solution 
is a fully integrated hardware –software platform that is ideal for gated -community and 
campusstyle deployment.    
1.1 Historical Background          
Artificial intelligence, computer vision and edge computing have made much ground in recent 
years, resulting in significant advances in the evolution of intelligent vehicle gate systems design. 
Manual credential checks, using RFID and barcode passes are sl ow, manual and insecure if lost, 
duplicated or accessed by an unauthorized user. The restrictions are more stringent in high traffic 
areas such as parking lots, neighborhood areas, offices, and facilities. It's not very efficient if there 
is delay and costs.    
To address these issues, contemporary smart gates are increasingly implementing Automatic 
Number Plate Recognition (ANPR) methods for a contactless way of identifying vehicles and 
authorizing entry. The first ANPR solutions relied on Optical Character Reco gnition (OCR) 
engines to recognize the license plate text after the localization of the license plate. In controlled 
conditions, OCR based methods would be effective but they tend to fail when there is a change of 
font types, variations in image quality, plate orientation, motion blur, and bad weather. In recent 
years, deep learning has advanced significantly and object detection techniques like YOLO have 
been introduced which can accurately detect the location of the license plate and can directly 
detect and classify each alphanumeric character without relying on traditional OCR engines.    
On the other hand, Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) have been recently integrated into 
Vehicle Make and Model Recognition (VMMR), another important scope of vehicle verification. 
These systems can be used to counter plate–vehicle swapping (or spoofing) attacks which are not \n===== PAGE 16 =====\n

possible to detect using just plate recognition. Reliable deployment outside is still challenging, 
due to the impact of the environmental conditions such as fog and low light, which can cause a 
significant degradation of image quality and recognition accuracy.    
The aim of this proposed project is to develop an All-Weather Intelligent Vehicle Gate Recognition 
System with the intelligent fusion of the proposed image enhancement algorithm of weatheraware 
image, dual-stage YOLOv8n License Plate Recognition (LPR), YOL Ov8n character and digit 
detection for 38 alphanumeric classes, and the EfficientNet -B4-based vehicle make model 
recognition for 245 vehicle categories. The system is fitted on Orange Pi 5 Pro edge platform and 
combined with a cloud-synchronized monitoring dashboard for a full endtoend solution for secure, 
automated and weather-proof vehicle access control.          
1.2 Objectives and Limitations          
The goal of this project is to investigate computer vision and deep learning powered automated 
vehicle access control. The essential purposes are:          
• Develop a real -time unit for YOLO based license plate detection for the purpose of license 
plate identification and location in vehicle image.          
• Learn to write an intelligent YOLOv8 pipeline for YOLO Character and Digit Detection and 
pre-processing techniques like denoising, histogram equalization, CLAHE, detail 
enhancement and sharpening.          
• Use EfficientNet-B0 for vehicle type detection - make and model classification.          
• Add authorization logic which cross -checks the text of the identified license plate and the 
vehicle model to an authorized vehicles store.          
• Evaluation of test performance under different conditions (day/night, including glare, fog, rain 
and motion blur) with above 90% accuracy.        
• Real-time gate processing, total system response time < 2 seconds (image capture to access 
decision using AI inference).      
    
    
Limitations:          
▪ Full industrial gate-motor integration is site-specific and beyond the scope of the academics; 
GPIO/LED gate indication is supported.    
▪ Working on Sun Glare Removal and Snow Removal from image.    
▪ However, the accuracy of recognition depends on the quality of the camera and weather 
conditions and can still be reduced in extreme weather conditions.    \n===== PAGE 17 =====\n

1.3 Project Scope          
This project offers a full chain smart vehicle gate recognition system encompassing model training, 
weather-sensitive preprocessing, edge deployment and cloud monitoring.    
Software scope includes:    
• The module for license plate detection is YOLOv8n.    
• YOLOv8n character and digit recognition (38 classes) with adaptive weather preprocessing 
( fog, rain, night )    
• Convolutional neural network EfficientNet-B4 for classification of vehicle make and model    
• (245 classes)    
• Permission control logic associated to a database of registered vehicles (allow / deny / 
temporary open/close gate) with automatic gate control decision    
• Web dashboard built using React + Node.js/Express and deployed on Vercel (Live Feed, 
event logs, vehicle registry, admin override)    
• Orange Pi integration – Pi processes all three AI models locally and uploads the results to 
the cloud using gate_sync.py.    
• Hardware scope includes:    
• Edge AI node: Orange Pi 5 Pro (16 GB)    
• 1080p camera with USB for images at the gate-lane    
• Secure upload of results to Vercel via Wi-Fi.    
• Optional GPIO/LED to indicate physically the gate status.    
• Precision, recall, mAP@0.5 and F1 are metrics used for the detection modules, while 
Top1/Top-5 accuracy is used for VMMR and the on-device end-to-end latency on the 
Orange Pi.    
1.4 Assumptions and Dependencies          
           Assumptions and dependencies of the project are:    
• On both development workstations (Google Colab/CUDA) and the Orange Pi 5 Pro edge 
node, the following software is installed and configured: Python, PyTorch, OpenCV , and 
Ultralytics YOLO. Model weights are shared through Hugging Face and loaded 
dynamically both by models_loader.py and the unified pipeline.py.    
• System Integration: All modules (YOLOv8n license plate detector, YOLOv8n 
character/digit recognizer, EfficientNet-B4 vehicle classifier, weather preprocessing, 
authorization logic and Vercel upload) are integrated into a single pipeline on Pi 
(pipeline.py), and cloud synchronization is performed by gate_sync.py.    
• Network and Security: Wi-Fi at deployment site for uploading detection events. A shared  
GATE_DEVICE_TOKEN authenticates Pi-to-cloud communication through the  
• Vercel's /api/gate/ingest endpoint.    
• Performance Assumption: The Orange Pi CPU can complete full inference (preprocess →    
• LPD (OCR → VMMR) within ≤ 2 seconds per captured frame (~700 – 1000ms measured).    
• Data Accuracy: The access decisions rely on a correct registry, which is maintained on the 
authorized vehicles, temporary visitors and allow/deny/temp policies through the web 
dashboard. Based on plate match and vehicle confidence the system returns gate_action: 
opened or closed.    
• Image Quality: Input images are expected to have a visible plate area and have enough 
surrounding vehicle to use for VMMR.  \n===== PAGE 18 =====\n

1.6 Thesis Key Contributions          
This research has two important contributions. First, it introduces an intelligent framework for the 
management of vehicles access control based on multiple artificial intelligence techniques such 
as   
Three key steps involved: YOLObased license plate localization, YOLO driven character and digit 
recognition, and Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) based vehicle make and model 
classification. The system consists of a combination of these elements that are integrated into a 
single software system, allowing for automated and efficient vehicle verification that can be 
deployed in real-world settings.    
Second, the study conducts an extensive evaluation of the proposed system under various 
operating conditions including different illuminations, reflections and moderate weather 
disturbances.    
1.6.1 Thesis Outline          
The theoretical background and literature review are presented in Chapter 2, focusing on 
automatic license plate recognition, vehicle make and model recognition and the intelligent access 
control system. It also points out the existing gaps and restrictions in approaches.    
The design and development of the proposed system is explained in chapter 3. Describes the 
general approach, system requirements, technologies chosen and the general system architecture 
for the solution.    
Chapter 4 explains the operation of the software modules, which include the license plate 
detection, character and digit detection, classification of vehicle makes and models, and access 
control system. System testing procedures, performance assessment and experimental results are 
also addressed in the chapter.    
Chapter 5 presents an analysis and discussion of the results obtained from the study, which 
includes the effectiveness, strengths and limitations of the proposed framework. A comparison is 
also made with existing solutions to assess the overall performance.    
The research findings and contribution are summarized in chapter 6. It also presents possible 
future upgrades, hardware integration options, and suggestions for making the system more 
capable and feasible..
